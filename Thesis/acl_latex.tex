\begin{abstract}
Evaluations of LLM-agent cooperation can conflate how much agents cooperate, how behavior changes with group size, and whether cooperation persists. We evaluate six models in 376 episodes of repeated homogeneous self-play, crossing two decision scaffolds with group sizes $N=2,3,4,5$. Three findings distinguish these measurements. Scaffold-associated group-size responses vary across models, with Llama the only individual interaction surviving Holm correction. Cooperation-level gaps need not imply supported slope differences: Mistral averages 80.24\% cooperation in free-form play versus 6.75\% under the structured scaffold, while its slope interaction remains uncertain. Early cooperation need not persist: DeepSeek's free-form cooperation falls from 39.87\% in rounds 1--5 to 2.79\% in rounds 26--30. This empirical case study motivates evaluating cooperation through separate measures of level, group-size response, and persistence, alongside model-specific uncertainty and execution reliability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Evaluating cooperation in LLM agents requires deciding what cooperation means as a measurement. An episode average measures cooperation level, but does not show how behavior responds to group size or whether initial cooperation persists. For LLM agents in repeated social dilemmas, cooperation level, group-size response, and persistence over rounds are distinct measurements; pooled means can hide model- and scaffold-specific differences.

This distinction matters because strategic behavior depends on the environment and its presentation. Classical group-size effects vary with benefit scaling \citep{scirep2015groupsize,plos2015curvilinear}, and state representation affects LLM behavior in strategic environments \citep{goodyear2025state}. A cooperation score therefore characterizes a model operating through a specified decision pipeline, rather than an intrinsic cooperative disposition.

We present an empirical evaluation case study of six named models in a repeated graded cooperation game. Across 376 fresh episodes, we cross $N\in\{2,3,4,5\}$ with two scaffolds. Both conditions provide the same state, histories, long-term reward objective, and output schema. The structured condition additionally supplies a constant ranking of immediate action payoffs favoring defection; free-form omits this ranking. The contrast concerns the salience of immediate incentives, not structured reasoning in general or chain-of-thought versus no reasoning. Episodes use homogeneous self-play and have no inter-agent communication channel.

The results distinguish scaffold-associated differences in cooperation level, group-size response, and persistence, with Llama the only individual interaction surviving Holm correction. The pooled H1/H2 tests provide no support for a simple universal scaling-defection account, one finding within this broader evaluation. Execution reliability qualifies what can be inferred from the observed trajectories.

Our contributions are (i) a controlled group-size-by-scaffold evaluation with episode-level uncertainty; (ii) a model-resolved decomposition of cooperation level, group-size response, and persistence; and (iii) an execution-reliability analysis identifying where pipeline failures limit behavioral interpretation. Pilot outcomes inform planning and are excluded from the main dataset. The evaluation targets measurement of agent behavior, rather than a universal scaling law or a communication mechanism.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Group size and social dilemmas.}
Classical work shows that cooperation need not decline monotonically with group size: the relationship depends on benefit scaling and saturation \citep{scirep2015groupsize,plos2015curvilinear}. Spatial public-goods games and reinforcement-learning settings extend the study of group-size effects to different interaction structures \citep{szolnoki2012spatial,rsos2024rlpgg}. These studies motivate specifying the payoff aggregation before interpreting a group-size response. Here, benefits depend on the average cooperation of others, rather than their summed contributions; the evaluation concerns this particular repeated environment.

\paragraph{Cooperation among LLM agents.}
\citet{piatti2024cooperate} study sustainable cooperation in societies of LLM agents, while \citet{piedrahita2025corrupted} examine free-riding by reasoning-focused models in public-goods games with costly sanctioning. These works locate cooperation within interacting agent populations. Our case study focuses on measurement within a fixed environment: separating episode-mean levels, scaffold-associated group-size responses, and round trajectories. The purposive model roster supports comparisons among the tested checkpoints, rather than a general taxonomy of reasoning capabilities.

\paragraph{Representation and behavioral evidence.}
\citet{goodyear2025state} study how state representation affects LLM-agent behavior in dynamic routing games. Our intervention instead adds an immediate-payoff ranking while retaining state information, histories, objectives, and the requested output schema. We measure its association with levels and group-size responses separately.

\section{The Environment}
\label{sec:env}

We use a repeated $N$-player graded cooperation game with endogenous
payoff dynamics and an optional perception-noise mechanism. The present
experiment fixes perception noise to zero. At each round $t$, every player $i$
simultaneously selects an action $a^t_i \in \{0, \dots, M-1\}$, mapped to
a cooperation level
\begin{equation}
  c^t_i = 1 - \frac{a^t_i}{M-1},
\end{equation}
so that $a=0$ is full cooperation and $a=M-1$ is full defection, with
$M-2$ graded intermediate levels. Player $i$'s reward in round $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  u^t_i = B^t_{\text{eff}} \cdot \bar{c}^t_{-i} - C \cdot c^t_i + K,
\end{equation}
where $\bar{c}^t_{-i} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j \neq i} c^t_j$ is the
\emph{average} cooperation of the other $N-1$ players. This averaging,
rather than summing, is the structural property that places this game on
the constant-benefit side of the classical group-size literature
(Section~\ref{sec:related}): a given player's own cooperation never
directly increases their own payoff, and the benefit available from
others does not grow with $N$, so each player's ability to influence the
shared outcome is diluted as the group grows.

Two endogenous mechanisms shape $B^t_{\text{eff}}$: a public cooperation
streak amplifies the benefit through a saturating bonus when group
cooperation exceeds $\theta$, and a slower drift process adjusts the base
benefit using recent cooperation. The engine supports perception noise,
but this study fixes $p=0$, so observed and true actions coincide.
Section~\ref{sec:design} gives the fixed parameters.

\subsection{Models, Conditions and Replication}
We test Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-3-12B-IT,
Mistral-Small-3.1-24B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B and
GPT-OSS-120B. The purposive roster spans developers, not a controlled
parameter-scale sweep or independent training lineages: DeepSeek's
checkpoint is distilled onto a Qwen base. DeepSeek and GPT-OSS form the
designated exploratory reasoning group. Qwen3 also supports thinking
\citep{qwen32025}, so this split is not a comprehensive capability taxonomy.

All seats in an episode share model and condition. The 48 cells cross
six models, four group sizes and two modes, with $M=5$, noise $p=0$,
threshold $\theta=.50$, and $T=30$. Fresh seeds per cell are unequal (2--16):
14/4/8/3/16/2 in the roster order, balanced across each model's eight cells:
376 episodes and 39,480 seat-round decisions. Main seeds start at 1001.
Pilot episodes are excluded, including GPT-OSS's pilot.
See Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-amendment} for the pilot-informed allocation,
power calculations and their limits.

The environment fixes $B_0=12$, $C=8$, $K=0$, $B_t\in[9,15]$,
drift window 8, $\eta=.35$, reference cooperation .55, streak threshold
.50, bonus scale $\lambda=.25$ and saturation parameter $\tau=4$.
Observation and summary histories contain at most ten prior rounds.
Agents use history-only memory and no opponent-prediction output. When the pipeline returns no valid decision, it supplies a fallback action.
There is no inter-agent communication channel.

Both prompts ask agents to maximize their own long-term average reward,
consider immediate and future consequences, and return the same JSON
schema. Structured play additionally receives a ranking by
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score}(a)=-C\,c(a).
\end{equation}
The opponent-benefit term cancels when comparing one's own actions.
Thus the ranking is always $[4,3,2,1,0]$, with scores $[0,-2,-4,-6,-8]$;
it does not depend on $N$, state or history. Agents are explicitly free
to reject the top-ranked action. Future streak and drift benefits are
not included in this ranking, although both modes receive their rules.
The contrast tests repeated salience of immediate defection incentives,
not an optimal repeated-game policy.

Serving configuration, hardware assignments, and output ceilings are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:execution}.

\subsection{Metrics and Analysis}

We analyze episode-mean cooperation and estimate how the free-form versus
structured group-size response differs within each model. The measurements are reported separately in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

The primary outcome is the mean normalized cooperation across every seat
and all 30 rounds of an episode. An episode is the behavioral analysis
unit. Reward, terminal streak and terminal benefit are secondary summaries;
first-attempt parse and fallback rates are separate reliability metrics.

H1 uses the structured-reference $N$ coefficient in the pooled Gaussian
identity-link mixed model $\bar C\sim N*\mathrm{free}$, fit to both modes
by maximum likelihood. Model identity supplies a random intercept and
$N$ slope, with an intercept-only alternative if no usable converged fit is
obtained. No episode random intercept is added to an episode-mean outcome.
H2 fits structured episodes only, comparing Gaussian flat
$\bar C=a$ and dilution $\bar C=a+b/(N-1)$ models by AIC/BIC.
Unequal replication affects the weighting of both pooled analyses.

Within each model, H4 estimates the free-form-minus-structured difference
in linear cooperation change from $N=2$ to $N=5$, using all four group
sizes, cell-specific variance estimates and Welch uncertainty; we report
marginal and six-model Holm-adjusted tests and an inverse-variance
heterogeneity test (see Appendix~\ref{sec:analysis-details}).

The existing pooled mixed-model interaction is secondary and descriptive;
it has no random $N$-by-mode interaction slope. Its estimate is distinct
from a population-power calculation that allows such heterogeneity.
The pooled fit converged using L-BFGS with the planned random intercept
and $N$ slope; an MLE boundary warning remains. No fitted prediction lies
outside $[0,1]$. We report these diagnostics and retain the planned specification without
post-hoc respecification.

\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Cooperation Level}
All 376 planned episodes completed and enter the primary analysis. Ten of 48 cells have zero observed episode variance; these floors constrain detection without establishing deterministic policies.

\textbf{Pooled means obscure model- and scaffold-specific differences in level, group-size response, and persistence.}
Full descriptive outcomes appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:outcomes};

Mean per-seat reward is 6.57 versus .13 in structured

\subsection{Group-Size Response}
\textbf{Scaffold effects on group-size responses vary across models.}
We estimated an endpoint-scale interaction for each model and tested their
heterogeneity. Only Llama survives six-model Holm correction
(Table~\ref{tab:main-h4}).

The six interactions reject the approximate shared-effect null:
$Q(5)=18.23$, $p=.0027$, $I^2=72.6\%$. This supports heterogeneity of
scaffold-associated group-size responses within the named roster.

Llama's endpoint-scale interaction is $+14.02$ percentage points
(95\% CI $[4.67,23.37]$, marginal $p=.004$, Holm $p=.024$).
Its free-form cooperation rises from 10.42\% at $N=2$ to 26.10\% at $N=5$,
while structured cooperation changes from 4.37\% to 5.54\%.
This positive interaction contradicts a uniform larger-group defection
narrative. Other individual intervals include zero.

GPT-OSS's interval is uninformative: two seeds per cell yield Welch degrees
of freedom 1.02 and an untruncated interval of $[-116.99,101.24]$ points.
Its main contrast variance coefficient is .01638 versus .0000934 in the
pilot. Pilot-based precision did not transport to this small main sample;
we do not use the realized effect to choose additional replication.

The secondary pooled mixed-model interaction is $+4.04$ endpoint-scale
points (95\% CI $[-2.72,10.80]$, $p=.242$).
It is descriptive and lacks a model-specific random interaction slope.
See Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-amendment} for pilot-based population power
ceilings and their distinction from the current fit. Heterogeneity limits
the scientific interpretation of pooling.

\paragraph{Pooled tests.}
H1 is not supported: the structured-reference $N$ coefficient is
$-0.040$ percentage points per added player (95\% CI $[-1.764,1.684]$,
$p=.964$). Figure~\ref{fig:main-coop} shows substantial structured floors,
including zero cooperation in every Gemma structured episode.
This result concerns the prespecified pooled linear slope.

H2 is also not supported. The flat model has AIC $-602.03$ and BIC
$-595.56$, versus $-600.29$ and $-590.58$ for dilution: flat-minus-dilution
$\Delta$AIC $=-1.74$ and $\Delta$BIC $=-4.98$.
The dilution coefficient $b=-.00616$ has the opposite sign to the proposed
decline. Together, H1/H2 provide no support for a simple universal scaling-defection account.

\subsection{Execution Reliability}

\textbf{Execution reliability is necessary to interpret cooperation.} Every one of the 48 cells passes the 12\% fallback ceiling
fixed before main collection (maximum observed cell rate 7\%), and the
primary analysis retains fallback-affected trajectories. Table~\ref{tab:main-reliability} reports first-attempt parse and fallback rates. The fallback action is full defection
(action~4; Appendix~\ref{sec:env-recurrences}), so fallbacks bias the affected
decisions toward defection rather than acting as a neutral default.

For Qwen, fallbacks occur on 0/1,680 structured and 100/1,680 free-form decisions (6.0\% free-form). A post-hoc saved-log check finds no fallbacks in its final five free-form rounds (0/280 decisions), all of which have zero cooperation. Thus late defection is not the direct substitution of fallback actions. Earlier fallback-affected histories still qualify strategic interpretation (Appendix~\ref{sec:qwen-log-check}). Gemma has no fallback, so its 3,360
structured defections cannot be attributed to the fallback rule.
DeepSeek has 0.03\%/0.10\% structured/free-form fallback. Llama has 0.4\%/1.3\% fallback and GPT-OSS
0.0\%/0.7\%. These rates characterize the tested pipeline, not intrinsic
strategic ability. Existing post-hoc first-attempt-valid and episode-filter checks for Qwen are summarized in Appendix~\ref{sec:qwen-log-check}; they do not replace the full-pipeline primary analysis.

Exploratory E1 shows no clean reasoning-group separation of interaction directions (Appendix~\ref{sec:e1}).

\section{Discussion}
This case study evaluates cooperation as a set of measurements conditioned on a model, scaffold, and execution pipeline. The level, response, and persistence results answer different questions. Their distinction is the contribution, rather than a universal law of scaling-induced defection.

The immediate-payoff ranking repeatedly highlights a unilateral incentive to defect while omitting future streak and drift benefits from its scores. Both conditions nevertheless receive the rules governing those benefits. The comparison therefore evaluates this particular scaffold and cannot identify an effect of structured reasoning generally.

Group size changes a player's marginal contribution to others' mean cooperation, aggregate histories, and threshold dynamics. Without frozen-benefit or opponent-composition controls, the responses cannot isolate diluted accountability, reciprocity, threshold coordination, or horizon reasoning. These remain possible interpretations rather than identified mechanisms. Qwen's late zero cooperation occurs without late fallbacks, but earlier fallback-affected histories prevent a clean separation of strategy from execution.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.92\columnwidth}
\textbf{Recommendations for evaluating agent cooperation.}
Report the exact scaffold and payoff representation, because the evaluated contrast changes decision presentation. Report per-model level and response estimates with uncertainty, because level gaps and slope interactions differ and replication is unequal. Report round trajectories, because early cooperation can disappear. Report fallback rates and the fallback action, because execution contributes to observed behavior.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\section{Conclusion}
Cooperation evaluation should distinguish level, group-size response, and persistence. This six-model case study illustrates why these measurements require model-specific uncertainty, a specified scaffold, trajectories, and execution reliability checks. The evidence supports a bounded evaluation contribution, rather than a universal account of scaling-induced defection.

\section*{Limitations}

The six purposively selected checkpoints are not a random sample of a
defined model population; training lineages overlap, and parameter count
is confounded with architecture, training and serving. The approximate
$Q$ test has only five heterogeneity degrees of freedom and uncertain
estimated variances. Welch uncertainty is particularly weak for GPT-OSS.
The Gaussian pooled model has floor-heavy outcomes and a boundary
warning; convergence and in-range predictions do not validate its
distributional assumptions. H1/H2 use unequal episode weighting across
models.

The pilot informed the design and variance-based budget. A hard compute
constraint made the original 10-point target infeasible; 15 points and a
25\% variance uplift were substituted before fresh collection. Several
models remain below 90\% prospective marginal power, and this uplift is
not a confidence bound. Null individual results do not exclude smaller
effects, and non-significant individual effects do not establish
equivalence. Planned marginal power is not family-wise power. Main and
pilot parsers differ for five models; no pilot outcomes are merged, but
variance transport remains uncertain. The amended primary family reports
both marginal and six-model Holm $p$-values; the additional heterogeneity
test is not included in that six-test correction.

Section~\ref{sec:results} and Appendix~\ref{sec:qwen-log-check} report pipeline reliability and post-hoc saved-log checks; first-attempt-valid subsets condition on successful generation and retain the observed histories. Passing the fallback gate does not remove fallback influence. Parse success does not guarantee action validity, and inference hardware and output-token ceilings vary across models.

This single English-language game uses homogeneous self-play, $N \le 5$,
30 rounds, one temperature, no communication and no human baseline. The
ranking is constant and myopic. There is no frozen-benefit or
opponent-matching ablation. Returned explanations are not hidden
reasoning. Temporal
observations are descriptive, not new confirmatory claims. Heterogeneity
does not establish every pairwise model difference or opposite causal
mechanisms. These limits preclude broad claims about morality, universal
reasoning-model behavior or the causal mechanism behind cooperation.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
The experiment uses simulated agent interactions and no human game
participants or personal data. Cooperation and defection are operational
action labels, not moral judgments or evidence of real-world intent.
The computational results do not establish general trustworthiness.

\section{Analysis Details}
\label{sec:analysis-details}
Within each model, H4 uses a linear-in-$N$ regression with mode and their
interaction. Define the endpoint-scale estimand
\begin{equation}
 D_m=3\bigl(\beta_{\mathrm{free},m}-\beta_{\mathrm{struct},m}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here $\beta_{\mathrm{free},m}$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{struct},m}$ are the respective linear-in-$N$ slopes. Positive $D_m$ means the free-form condition changes more positively, or
declines less, between $N=2$ and $N=5$. All four $N$ levels contribute;
this is not the literal two-endpoint difference. With cell episode
variance $v_{m,r,N}$ and $s_m$ seeds per cell,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 q_m&=\sum_{r,N}[0.6(N-3.5)]^2v_{m,r,N},\\
 \mathrm{Var}(\widehat D_m)&=q_m/s_m.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The endpoint multiplier gives weights $3(N-3.5)/5=0.6(N-3.5)$; the mode contrast has opposite signs, which disappear on squaring in Eq.~(5). The implementation combines OLS point estimates with cell-specific
variances and Welch--Satterthwaite $t$ uncertainty. Main-data variances
are re-estimated; pilot variances are planning inputs only. Report
two-sided marginal $p$-values at $\alpha=.05$ and supplemental Holm-adjusted
$p$-values; planned marginal power is not family-wise power.

H4 heterogeneity uses inverse-variance weights $w_m$ and
$Q=\sum_m w_m(\widehat D_m-\bar D_w)^2$; report the approximate
$\chi^2_5$ reference and $I^2=\max(0,(Q-5)/Q)$ (zero when $Q=0$).
Six models and estimated standard errors limit calibration. Significant
$Q$ indicates heterogeneity, not a proof of inadequate power.

\section{Environment Recurrences and Execution Seeds}
\label{sec:env-recurrences}
Let $g_t=N^{-1}\sum_i c_i^t$, $s_0=0$ and $B_1=12$.
The current payoff uses
\begin{equation}
 B_{\mathrm{eff}}^t=B_t[1+.25\tanh(s_{t-1}/4)].
\end{equation}
After actions, $s_t=s_{t-1}+1$ if $g_t\geq.5$ and zero otherwise.
Let $r_t$ be the average of the available $g$ values over the last eight
rounds including $t$. Drift updates
\begin{equation}
 B_{t+1}=\mathrm{clip}_{[9,15]}[B_t+.35(r_t-.55)].
\end{equation}
With zero perception noise these observed cooperation means are true means.
The base benefit is bounded by 15; the streak-amplified effective benefit
can exceed 15. Observations expose current benefit, streak, rolling state,
prior ten-round action histories and action frequencies, never simultaneous
current opponent actions. Generation seeds are derived from phase,
episode identity (including condition) and seat, so equal episode seed
labels across modes do not imply paired random generations.
Fallback maximizes $-C\,c(a)$ with a cooperative tie-break; at $C=8$ its
unique choice is action~4 (full defection).
